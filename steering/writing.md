# Writing

Read this before writing anything a person outside the company will read: an
email, a proposal, a post, a page, release notes. Not for code, specs or
internal notes, where precision beats voice.

## Your voice

The company's voice lives in `company/COMPANY.md`, under *How we sound*. Read
it first. If it's empty, ask for three words that describe how the company
writes and one example they like, and suggest adding them there.

## What makes writing read as written by a person

- **Specific over abstract.** Real numbers, real examples, real tools. Not
  "streamlined workflows", but "the report that took two hours now takes ten
  minutes".
- **Lead with the point.** Context after, if it's needed at all.
- **Vary the rhythm.** Short sentences to make a point. Longer ones to explain
  something. A page of sentences all the same length reads as generated.
- **Write to the reader.** "You get", "your team owns", rather than "the company
  provides".
- **Name who acts.** "We tested it Wednesday", not "testing was conducted".
  Never make "the system", "the process" or "the approach" the subject.
- **End with a next step or a real question**, not a recap.

## Cut on sight

- **Hype and filler:** leverage, cutting-edge, empower, revolutionise, synergy,
  best-in-class, holistic, paradigm, unlock value, game-changer, "in today's
  fast-paced world", "at the end of the day".
- **Announcing instead of saying:** "Here's why", "The key point is", "The truth
  about", "What nobody tells you". Just say it.
- **Hedges you don't mean:** may, might, could, seems, arguably, perhaps,
  generally. Keep one only where you're actually unsure.
- **False choices:** "It's not X, it's Y." Say how the two actually relate.
- **Unsourced authority:** "Studies show", "experts agree". Name the source, or
  own the claim.
- **Em dashes.** They're the most common tell of machine-written text. Use a
  colon, commas, parentheses, or two sentences.

## Lengths that work

| Kind | Length |
|---|---|
| Email to a customer | 3 to 5 sentences. The point first, one clear ask |
| Social post | 150 to 250 words. A first line that earns the second |
| Blog post | 1,000 to 2,000 words. One idea, opened with the problem |

## Before you hand it over

Read it back once as the person receiving it. Cut any sentence that could
appear unchanged in any other company's writing. Then score it 0 to 10 on
directness, rhythm, trust in the reader, voice, and density. Under 8 on any of
them, rewrite that part.
